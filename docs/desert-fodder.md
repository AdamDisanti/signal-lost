# Desert fodder enemies

Five small enemies for the Desert / Dunes biome. Each one is built around a piece of junk from the ship, and each has a different job from the hermit crab (melee rusher) and the mortar frog (goo lobber). They keep spawning around the [desert mini-bosses](desert-minibosses.md), and also work on their own. Owner: Lawson. All models are low-poly placeholders generated by the builder script, with no imported assets. All numbers are placeholders to tune.

This feature lives entirely in new files. It does not change the tutorial, the Desert Dunes scene, the shared player, scanner or HUD prefabs, or any existing enemy or script.

## Open the delivered assets

The local `tutorial-zone` integration now includes the **saved prefabs, meshes, materials and all six fodder test scenes**; there is no generation step needed to see them. Open `Assets/Scenes/DesertFodderTest.unity`, or a single-enemy scene from the table below, and press Play. Prefabs are under `Assets/Prefabs/Enemies/Desert/`. The existing `EnemyTest.unity` remains the original crab test, not the fodder arena.

## Create the prefabs and test scene

For a checkout that contains only the scripts, or to reproduce the assets in a disposable project:

1. Open the `signal-lost/` project in Unity **6000.6.2f1** with `feature/desert-fodder` merged and wait for scripts to compile.
2. Choose **Signal Lost > Desert Fodder > Create Test Scene**.
3. The Console should print `DESERT_FODDER_TEST_SCENE_CREATED_AND_VALIDATED`, and `Assets/Scenes/DesertFodderTest.unity` opens.

It creates:

- `Assets/Prefabs/Enemies/Desert/`: `SparkTick`, `SnorkelEel`, `JerryCanBeetle`, `HubcapArmadillo` and `DishMeerkat` prefabs, plus their meshes in `Meshes/`.
- `Assets/Materials/Enemies/Desert/`: their materials.
- `Assets/Scenes/DesertFodderTest.unity`: the mixed test, a 100 x 100 m sandy arena with rocks and hull plates, the player (same setup as the Desert Dunes level: laser, 100 HP, placeholder respawn, scanner on **Q**, HUD), and five spawner stations.

Commit all of these, with their `.meta` files: prefabs and meshes under `Assets/Prefabs/Enemies/Desert/`, materials under `Assets/Materials/Enemies/Desert/`, scenes under `Assets/Scenes/`. The menu refuses to run again once the scene exists. **Signal Lost > Desert Fodder > Regenerate Prefabs** rebuilds the five prefabs and meshes from the script with the same GUIDs, throwing away edits made to them. **Validate Test Scene** re-runs the checks.

## The enemies

### Spark Tick (swarm)

A palm-sized sand tick with stripped copper wire sticking out of its back. Ticks come in packs, zigzag toward you, and leap from about 4 m away. If a leap hits, the tick latches onto your visor (you see it on screen) and deals 2 damage every 0.5 s.

- **Counterplay:** one hit of anything kills a tick on the ground (8 HP), so the wide side bash clears a pack.
- **Shaking them off:** latched ticks can't be hit. Each melee swing swats off up to two of them. Jumping throws them all off, and they land dazed.
- **Dodging:** sidestep a leap and the tick lands dazed.
- At most four can latch on at once.

### Snorkel Eel (burrower)

A sand eel breathing through a bent exhaust pipe.

- **Burrowed:** it swims under the sand toward you. Only the pipe and a moving sand mound show.
- **Burst:** within about 2.4 m, the sand bulges and shakes for 0.7 s (your warning), then it bursts up and bites for 14 if you are still within 1.9 m.
- **Recovery:** it stays exposed for 1.4 s, then dives again.
- **Counterplay:** while it is buried, only the pipe can be hit. Hit the pipe with a bolt or a swing and the eel is yanked out, stunned on the surface for 2.4 s, and takes half the hit. Otherwise, step out of the bulge and hit it while it is exposed. 50 HP.
- **Scanner:** each eel has an `EnemySignalSource`, so the scanner tracks it while it is buried.

### Jerry-Can Beetle (walking bomb)

A fat, slow beetle hauling a leaky red fuel can. It waddles straight at you at 2.2 m/s.

- **Fuse:** within 2.4 m it stops, swells, and hisses while the can blinks faster and faster for 1.2 s. Then it explodes: 32 damage at the centre, falling to 30% at the 3.6 m edge.
- **The blast hurts everything:** enemies, other beetles and you.
- **Killed early:** a dead beetle still blows up 0.45 s later. Shoot them from range, or near other enemies to use them as a crowd clearer.
- **Chain reactions:** blasts and fire ignite beetles (0.5 s fuse), so packs chain-react. 30 HP.

### Hubcap Armadillo (rolling charger)

An armadillo with a chrome ship hubcap fused into each side of its shell, so it rolls like a wheel.

- **Spacing:** from 5-15 m it curls up and spins in place for 0.75 s (your warning). If you are closer than 5 m, it backs off first.
- **Roll:** it rolls at you at 11 m/s, curving slightly toward you early on, and bounces off walls, rocks and hull plates up to twice. A hit deals 18 and shoves you back.
- **While curled:** laser bolts glance off the hubcap with 40% of their damage. They can bounce back toward you.
- **Missed roll:** sidestep and it skids to a stop and uncurls dizzy for 2 s. That's your opening.
- **Flip:** a heavy hit of 20+ damage (the overhead slam) while it is curling, rolling, skidding or dizzy flips it onto its back for 3 s. 70 HP.

### Dish Meerkat (spotter / support)

A meerkat wearing a tiny radar dish. It never attacks.

- **Shriek:** it pops out of a burrow, watches you for 0.8 s, stands tall with the dish whirring for 0.8 s (your warning), then shrieks.
- **Spotted:** for 6 s, every desert fodder enemy within 40 m knows where you are (walls don't matter) and moves 25% faster. A red **SPOTTED!** banner with a timer and red screen edges shows it.
- **Jamming:** while it is popped up and you are within 12 m, its radar jams your scanner. The arrow disappears, the meter flickers, static plays instead of beeps, and **SCANNER JAMMED** shows.
- **Counterplay:** it is fragile (20 HP) and can only be hit while popped up. When hit, it ducks down, then pops up from a different hole nearby. Killing meerkats first makes a fight easier and frees your scanner.

## Shared systems

- **`FodderSpawner`:** put it at the centre of an area with a NavMesh and list the prefabs to spawn, with a weight and a group size for each. While the player is within its activation range, it spawns a wave every few seconds at the edge of its area, preferring spots behind the player, up to a cap on how many are alive at once. Assign **Stop When Dead** (for example a mini-boss's `Health`) to stop spawning when that dies; the fodder already out keeps fighting. It also exposes `Killed` and an `EnemyKilled` event for encounter or objective counting later.
- **`SpotterAlert`:** the meerkat's "spotted" state, as a static flag any script can read (`SpotterAlert.IsMarked`). The mini-bosses can use it later. Dying clears it.
- **`ScannerJamReceiver`:** jams Amanda's scanner from the outside. While a jammer is in range, it switches `ScannerController` off (so the arrow and beeps stop), adds meter noise and static, and switches it back on afterwards. It adds itself to the scene's scanner automatically the first time a meerkat appears, so no prefab or scene change is needed. Any script can be a jammer by implementing `IScannerJammer`.
- **`FodderExplosion` and `IIgnitable`:** a blast that damages every `Health` in range once, with falloff, and ignites anything `IIgnitable`. The future fuel scorpion's oil fires can call `Ignite` on beetles.
- **`FodderStatusOverlay`:** **PLACEHOLDER** on-screen text (SPOTTED, SCANNER JAMMED, ticks latched on), drawn without touching the shared HUD prefab. It is created automatically when needed. Replace it during the UI pass.
- **`DesertFodder`:** the base class: finding the player, line of sight, the spotted alert, hit flash, knockback and the default death. Like the crab and frog, each enemy's NavMeshAgent starts disabled and is switched on in `Start` once the NavMesh is built.

## Scripts

All runtime scripts are in `Assets/Scripts/Enemies/Desert/` (namespace `SignalLost.Enemies.Desert`):

- `DesertFodder.cs`: shared base
- `SparkTick.cs`, `SnorkelEel.cs`, `JerryCanBeetle.cs`, `HubcapArmadillo.cs`, `DishMeerkat.cs`: the five enemies
- `FodderSpawner.cs`, `SpotterAlert.cs`, `ScannerJamReceiver.cs` (with `IScannerJammer`), `FodderExplosion.cs` (with `IIgnitable`), `ExpandingRing.cs`, `FodderStatusOverlay.cs`: shared systems

The builder is `Assets/Editor/DesertFodderBuilder.cs`. It reuses the public helpers in `EnemyTestBuilder` and `LowPolyMesh` without changing them.

## Manual acceptance checks

1. Run **Create Test Scene**. No Console errors, and the validation message appears. Press Play. No errors; there may be a short pause while the NavMesh builds.
2. **Ticks (north):** a pack zigzags in. Bash one pack: one swing kills several.
   - Let one leap on you: it clings on screen, the HUD flashes red every half second, and the hint shows.
   - Swing: up to two pop off. Jump: the rest fall off and sit dazed for a moment.
   - Sidestep a leap: it lands dazed.
3. **Eels (east):** raise the scanner (**Q**): it points at a buried eel. Lower it.
   - Watch a pipe and sand mound slide toward you. When the sand bulges, step away: the burst misses.
   - Stand still: you are bitten for 14.
   - Shoot the pipe while it is buried: the eel pops out stunned. Hit it while it is exposed.
4. **Beetles (south-east):** let one reach you: it stops, swells and blinks faster, then explodes, and you take damage if you're close.
   - Shoot one from range: it pops a moment later.
   - Shoot one standing next to another: both go off in a chain.
5. **Armadillos (south-west):** from mid range it curls and spins, then rolls at you.
   - Get hit: 18 damage and a shove.
   - Sidestep: it skids and wobbles dizzy. Hit it.
   - Shoot it while it rolls: the bolt glances off with a spark.
   - Slam it (third combo hit) while it is dizzy or rolling: it flips onto its back.
   - Watch it bounce off rocks.
6. **Meerkats (west):** one pops up, watches, stands tall, and shrieks with a red ring. **SPOTTED!** appears with a timer, and the ticks there rush you from further away, faster.
   - Raise the scanner near a popped-up meerkat: **SCANNER JAMMED**, no arrow, flickering meter, static. Walk away or kill it: the scanner works again.
   - Hit a meerkat without killing it: it ducks, then pops up somewhere else nearby.
7. Walk away from a station: it stops spawning. No station ever has more than its cap alive.
8. Die: you respawn at the start, the SPOTTED state is cleared, and no ticks are left clinging to you.
9. Exit and re-enter Play Mode, and repeat a quick fight. The existing test scenes, the tutorial and the desert level are unchanged.

## Open questions

- **Scrap:** the design says enemies drop scrap. No scrap system exists yet, so fodder drops nothing.
- **Frogs and crabs when spotted:** being spotted only affects the new desert fodder. Making the frog aim better or the crab notice you while spotted would mean editing Christian's scripts. Confirm with him first.
- **Placing them in the Desert Dunes level:** Nicole owns that scene. Adding spawners to its encounter sites or mini-boss arenas is a separate, coordinated change.
- **Eel and scanner:** the eel is tracked as a normal above-ground signal, because the scanner skips buried and underground sources. If Amanda adds real underground detection, flip the eel's signal settings.
- **Death reset:** fodder stays dead and spawners keep their state when the player dies (the placeholder respawn doesn't reset enemies).
