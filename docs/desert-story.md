# Desert story: the Chop Shop and the Core Worm

Lawson, 11-08: set up the desert with a short run of quests. Each one has the player do a task and then fight one of the three [mini-bosses](desert-minibosses.md) in its own themed arena. Once all three are beaten and the last trophy is on its stand, the [Core Worm](desert-boss.md) bursts out, grabs the player and drags them into a large underground arena. After it is beaten and its trophy picked up, a gash in the roof choked with broken rock gives way, behind a flashing red crush zone. Sand pours in as a debris cone and the rocks tumble down it. The fall is a recorded physics simulation, so it is the same every time and always leaves a way out. The player climbs the rubble out into daylight.

Everything is generated by code; no asset packs were imported. All numbers and lines are placeholders to tune.

## How it plays

Cavendish's camp is by the arrival dock (see [islands](islands.md)). He gives every job, and the scanner leads to whatever the current objective needs.

| Job | Task (three of each) | Mini-boss and arena | Trophy |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Picked Clean** | Pick up Old Buzzard's moulted feathers along the dunes west of camp | **Old Buzzard** at its **Roost** in the Wreck Field (west) | Old Buzzard's Tail Feathers |
| **Burning Rubber** | Smother the fires Burnout left on its way east (E puts each out with sand) | **Burnout** at the **Scorch Raceway** in the Mesa Pass (east) | Burnout's Thorn Crest |
| **Oil and Trouble** | Seal the three leaking fuel lines round the sump | **Sludge** crawls out of its **Fuel Sump** at the Buried Hull (north-west) | Sludge's Stinger |

1. **Welcome.** Talk to Cavendish. He explains that three thieves, the Chop Shop, took the parts bolted round the Engine Core, and that he has put up a new rack by the fire for a trophy from each. That gives the first job.
2. **Each job:**
   1. Do the three tasks. Each one is E on the spot, with a quest signal for the scanner.
   2. The mini-boss only appears once its task is done. It fights exactly as before: its fodder, two phases, and resetting if you leave or die.
   3. Beaten, it drops its stolen ship part (walk over it) and a trophy (E to pick up).
   4. Set the trophy on the **Chop Shop rack** at camp.
   5. Talk to Cavendish: he takes the part "for safe keeping" and gives the next job.
3. **The worm.**
   1. Setting the third trophy on the rack springs the Core Worm. The ground shakes, it bursts out of the sand beside the camp, roars and swallows you.
   2. You are dragged into its **lair**, a cavern 22 m under its old sand bowl. It bursts out again, and the fight runs as in the test scene: four phases, the sandstorm, the collapsing floor, the meltdown, and the flask on F.
   3. **If you die,** you respawn on the surface. The worm lurks under the camp again, and walking back to the rack springs it again.
4. **The way out.**
   1. Beaten, the worm leaves the **Engine Core** and a **Core Worm Fang**.
   2. Once both are picked up, the ceiling comes down (11-09, Lawson's third redesign: it plays out the same every time, with physics-faithful rubble, looks natural, and always leaves a way out):
      - A long gash in the roof on the north side has been choked with broken rock since the fight began; you can see it up there.
      - Now the floor where it will land flashes **red**, faster and faster, for 4.5 s. The roof rumbles and sheds grit.
      - The rock face holding it gives way. The dunes above pour through as a curtain of sand and pile into a **debris cone** under the gash.
      - About 220 angular blocks, slabs and shards of roof rock come down onto it. They bounce off the gash's walls, the pillars and each other, tumble down the cone in puffs of dust and come to rest.
      - It is a recording of a real physics simulation, so every knock is physically right and it lands exactly the same way every time. The whole fall lasts 14 s.
      - Anyone still in the red zone is **crushed** (killed). You respawn on the surface.
      - The gash is left open to the sky, and daylight pours in.
   3. Climb the rubble, between the rocks, and you step out into the worm's old sand bowl.
   4. It all stays where it fell, so you can go back down into the lair and up again.
5. **Home.** Set the fang on a stand, then talk to Cavendish for the wrap-up. The Engine Core has cleared the desert, so the boat at the north dock sails on, to a Fog Forest that isn't built yet.

## The arenas

They are part of `DesertEnvironment.prefab`, so the NavMesh walks round them. Each sits on a 24 m flattened site with gaps toward camp.

- **Old Buzzard's Roost (Wreck Field):**
  - open sand under the sky for its dives;
  - a ring of low hull plates to duck behind when it bombs;
  - a broken ring of boulders round the edge;
  - bare dead trees for perches;
  - a big skull, ribs and loose feathers;
  - its nest of sticks and ship scrap, with three eggs, on a rock stump across from the way in.
- **Burnout's Scorch Raceway (Mesa Pass):**
  - an oval track with a banked sandstone berm all round, edged with red-and-white kerbs to bank off;
  - four rock pillars in the infield to crash into;
  - scorched skid marks and burnt patches;
  - a chequered start gate across the way in;
  - a pit canopy and stacks of fuel drums.
- **Sludge's Fuel Sump (Buried Hull):**
  - a 1.4 m dip of oily sand round a huge half-buried fuel tank;
  - pipes from the tank to the three leaks;
  - a drilling derrick;
  - hull slabs and boulders spread round for cover from the fuel cannon;
  - pools of oil, drums, and a rubble ring with two ways in.

## The lair

`CorewormLair.prefab`, under the boss bowl, centred on (31, 84):

- **The floor:** the Core Worm's arena, 68 m across. Its 5 m tiles still fall away in phase 3.
- **Walls and roof:** a boulder wall, with a lumpy rock dome over everything.
- **Cover:** six cave pillars from floor to roof, and three slabs of swallowed hull.
- **Light:** glowing crystal clusters with warm lights, and old bones round the walls.
- **The gash:** a slot in the roof 17 m long and 11 m wide, running from 27 m north of the middle (its **lip**, where the climb comes out) back toward the middle.
  - **The hole in the dunes** (11-10, after Lawson saw jagged edges and gaps down to the bottom of the map) is cut exactly along the gash's outline, 0.6 m outside it. The terrain triangles near it are split into 0.5 m pieces first, so its edge is a smooth curve, not the terrain's 4 m triangles.
  - **Its walls** start on that very edge, built from the same points the dunes are cut along, so there is no gap between sand and wall.
    - At the top the sand rolls over a rounded brink, in the dunes' own material where it meets them (flat sand or slope).
    - It turns to sandstone about half a metre down, then to the cavern's rock about 3 m down.
    - From there it flares out to meet the opening in the cavern roof.
    - Rocks bounce off the walls, and they keep the player climbing the debris inside them.
  - The lip comes out on the bowl's gently sloping floor, clear of the engine wreck; the bowl's rim is too steep to step out onto.
  - **The lid:** the patch of dunes over the gash (the environment's **Sinkhole Lid**) drops away when it gives way, leaving the gash open to the sky.
- **The debris cone:** what the sand piles into. It is shaped like a talus cone leaning against the wall it fell from: level with the dunes at the lip, then falling away in every direction at 33°, lumpy, and held under the roof outside the gash.
  - It looks like a rubble field: rough broken ground packed with about 2,200 low angular stones, plus 260 bigger half-buried ones away from the route.
  - What you walk on is the smooth surface underneath, so the low stones never trip you.
  - It grows the way poured sand does: a cone under the pour whose top rises and foot spreads, over 2.6 s.
- **The rocks:** about 220 blocks, slabs, chunks, shards and wedges (ten shapes, 0.6–3.2 m), each a squared-off lump sliced by random flat cuts. The cut faces are paler, like fresh breaks.
  - They start packed in the gash under a cracked rock face, above where the cone's surface will be and inside its walls.
  - **Packed in sand** (11-10, after Lawson saw rocks floating in the gash): sand fills the gash round them, so from the cavern the gash is a sandy ceiling with rocks bedded in it.
    - Its underside follows the rocks: nowhere lower than the middle of the lowest rock near, so every rock is at least half buried and none hangs in the air.
    - When the roof gives way it crumbles patch by patch, each patch just as the rocks in it come loose (the recording knows when). In a patch the clumps break off a moment apart, drop and crumble to nothing as they fall.
  - They come loose from the gash's far end toward the lip, the lowest first.
- **The recording** (`CorewormCollapse.asset`, a `CollapseBake`):
  - The level builder copies the lair into a scene with its own physics and makes the rocks rigid bodies. It lets them fall onto the cone, the floor, the pillars and each other until they rest, at 60 steps a second.
  - It then checks the result and re-runs it without the offenders until it is clean:
    - no rock lost out of the cavern or sunk into the cone;
    - a walking route from the cavern floor up the cone and over the lip, between the settled rocks. If rocks block every way, the fewest that would open one are left out of the next run.
  - A spot counts as walkable if it is on the cone, has headroom, has nothing solid standing in it, and is no steeper than 43° from the next spot.
  - The clean run is stored: each rock's position and rotation 30 times a second, and the hard knocks, which drive the dust puffs and tremors.
  - Rebuilding gives the same recording, because the build is seeded.
  - In the game the rocks are moved along their recorded paths. No physics runs, so it cannot come out differently. They are solid again once they rest.
- **The red zone:** flashing red with a brighter edge and a red light. Being in it while the fall is dangerous kills.
  - It covers the floor where the debris ends up more than 4 m deep, plus everywhere a rock lands or rolls (the recording knows).
  - **It always leaves room to stand** (11-09, after it covered the whole arena): it is 53% of the arena floor in this build, all on the gash's side.
    - Nothing comes within 10 m of the middle on the far (south) side. The recording leaves out any rock that strays past that line, and the rocks roll less (more damping). This build needed 13 runs and keeps 184 rocks.
    - The build fails if the zone crosses the line or covers more than 55% of the floor.
  - The cone's shallow skirt is outside the zone. Someone standing there is lifted onto the debris as it settles, not buried.
- **Cave light:** while you are down there, `WormLair` dims the sun and the sky light, and the fog turns dark and dusty. It eases back to daylight as you climb out (or respawn on the surface).

## Trophies and the rack

- **The new trophies:** four, built in code into `Resources/SignalLost/Trophies` and listed in `CampSave`'s `Trophy` table:
  - the tail feathers, the thorn crest and the stinger;
  - the Core Worm Fang.
- **The rack:** the desert camp now has the **Chop Shop rack**, three more stands east of the fire under a driftwood sign. The camp save remembers them like the first three, as stands 4-6.
- **What goes where:** any trophy goes on any free stand. The rack is where the scanner points while you carry a Chop Shop trophy. The fang goes on any free stand.
- **Saved from an earlier session:** if a trophy is already on a stand from an earlier session, beating its boss doesn't drop another, and the job counts it as placed.

## Build it

- **Signal Lost > Desert > Install Desert Story** does the whole thing:
  - rebuilds `DesertEnvironment.prefab` (the arenas, the Sump's dip and the sinkhole lid);
  - rebuilds the desert camp (with the rack), the trophies and `CorewormLair.prefab`;
  - installs everything in `DesertDunes.unity`.
  - The scene gets a **Desert Story** object: the story, the three mini-bosses with their fodder spawners (hidden until their jobs), the nine tasks, the Core Worm, its lair and the ambush at the rack.
  - It removes the old hermit-crab encounters and the boss and Engine Core placeholders.
  - Run it again to rebuild; it replaces its own earlier install.
- **Signal Lost > Desert > Validate Desert Story** checks the wiring.

Code:

- `Scripts/Story/DesertStory.cs`: the quest flow.
- `Scripts/Story/QuestTask.cs`: an E task that hides or shows parts of itself and puts out its fire.
- `Scripts/World/WormLair.cs`: the cave light, the collapse and the climb.
- `Scripts/World/CollapseBake.cs`: the recorded rockfall and its playback.
- `Editor/DesertDunesStory.cs`: the arenas, the lair, the trophies and the install.

Small changes to existing code:

- `DesertStageExit` can wait for a story to arm it, and can skip its "back to the exit" ending.
- `Coreworm.UseGroundHeight` lets the worm hide under the camp's sand while its arena is far below.
- The desert terrain has the Sump's dip and the lid cut out of it.
- The spires leave the way out of the sinkhole clear.

1. Load `DesertDunes` (or sail from the tutorial). Cavendish's quest signal is on, and the three bosses are nowhere to be seen.
2. Talk to him: the welcome ends with "Picked Clean". The scanner leads to the feathers; E picks each up.
3. Old Buzzard appears at its roost. Fight it, then pick up the feathers trophy and the wing panel.
4. At camp the scanner points at the rack. Place the trophy, then talk to Cavendish: "Burning Rubber".
5. Put out the three fires; Burnout is on the raceway. Repeat for Sludge: seal the leaks, and it engages right away in the sump.
6. Place the stinger: the ground shakes, and the worm erupts east of camp and swallows you.
7. In the cavern it is dim and orange-lit. Die once: you are back on the surface in daylight. Walk to the rack: swallowed again.
8. Beat the worm. Pick up the Engine Core and the fang:
   - The north part of the floor flashes red under the choked gash.
   - Stay in it and you're crushed and respawn up top. Step out and watch the sand pour and the rocks tumble down.
   - Do it twice (the stage-skip testing tool gets you back quickly): every rock lands in the same place both times.
9. Walk up the rubble into the bowl, between the rocks, with the sky showing through the gash. Daylight returns.
   - Walk back down into the lair and up again: the rocks stay where they stopped.
   - Go home, place the fang and talk to Cavendish: the desert is cleared, and the north dock's boat is open.

## My calls (open to change)

- **Order:** the jobs are in a fixed order: Old Buzzard, Burnout, then Sludge. Each mini-boss stays hidden until its job, so they can't be fought out of order or early.
- **The tasks:** feathers, fires and leaks are my picks, each themed on its boss and leading to its arena.
- **The rack:** the desert camp gets three more stands for the Chop Shop's trophies rather than sharing the three boss stands. The tutorial's camp still has three, so it doesn't show stands 4-6.
- **When the worm comes:** it is sprung by placing the third trophy. The Engine Core's ship part doesn't need to be handed in first.
- **The worm's trophy:** a fang, dropped near the middle of the lair a few seconds after its death throes.
- **The camp-side ambush:** it replaces the old idea of the worm springing at the stage exit. The departure dock still needs the Engine Core.
- **Removed:** the old hermit-crab encounter sites are gone; their places are now the arenas.
- **Save:** progress is per Play session, like the rest of the game, apart from the trophies.
- **The collapse (third redesign, recorded physics):**
  - the 4.5 s warning;
  - the crush kills outright, rather than doing heavy damage;
  - **Recorded, not live.** "Always the same" and "physics faithful" together mean simulating once and playing it back. Live physics differs from run to run.
  - **The cone is shaped, not simulated.** Rocks alone can't be relied on to pile 22 m up to the gash. The cone carries the climb, and the rocks falling onto it are the simulated part.
  - **Rocks that would block the way out are left out of the fall** (4 of 223 in this build), rather than moved afterwards, so nothing in the recording is faked.
  - **The sand pours first,** then the rocks come loose, so no rock lands before the cone under it is there.
  - **The fall is cut off at 14 s;** anything still creeping then stops where it is.
- **Open:**
  - The cone's mesh is a 12 MB text asset (three stone-field meshes in the repo are the same size). Fewer or simpler stones would shrink it.
  - Whether a rolling rock should hurt a player it passes outside the red zone. It can't today: the zone covers every rock's path.
  - Sound for the collapse.
